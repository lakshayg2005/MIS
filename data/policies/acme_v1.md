# Acme Technologies - Employee Transport Policy
Version: 1
Effective: 2026-01-01

## 1. Eligibility

### 1.1 Grade and distance
Employees in grade L3 and above are eligible for office transport. Employees in grades L1 and L2 are eligible only if their home is more than 8 km from the office. Interns are not eligible for transport.

### 1.2 Shift-based eligibility
Employees rostered on a shift that starts before 6:00 AM or ends after 8:00 PM are eligible for transport irrespective of grade or distance.

## 2. Booking

### 2.1 Cut-off timings
A cab or shuttle seat must be booked at least 4 hours before the shift start (pickup) or shift end (drop). Requests inside the 4 hour window are treated as ad-hoc trips and need approval under section 5.

### 2.2 Cancellation
A booking can be cancelled free of charge up to 2 hours before the scheduled time. A later cancellation is treated as a no-show.

## 3. Night shift and safety

### 3.1 Security escort
A security escort is mandatory for every woman employee on drops after 8:00 PM. The transport desk assigns the escort automatically when the drop is booked. The employee does not need to request it.

### 3.2 Night drop timings
Drops between 8:00 PM and 6:00 AM are operated only on fixed routes. The last drop of the night leaves the office at 1:00 AM.

### 3.3 Safety reporting
Any safety concern during a trip must be reported to the transport desk helpline immediately. The driver is not allowed to change the route without informing the employee.

## 4. Weekends and holidays

### 4.1 Weekend transport
Weekend pickups and drops are available on Saturday and Sunday only with the reporting manager's approval, raised at least 24 hours in advance.

### 4.2 Public holidays
Transport on a declared public holiday is provided only for employees in critical support rosters listed by the department head.

## 5. Ad-hoc trips and approvals

### 5.1 Ad-hoc trip approval
An ad-hoc trip (outside the roster or inside the booking cut-off) is approved by the reporting manager. Approval must be recorded before the trip starts.

### 5.2 Airport trips
Airport pickups and drops need approval from the department head. The reporting manager cannot approve an airport trip.

### 5.3 Cost cap
An ad-hoc cab trip is capped at Rs 1,500 per trip. Anything above the cap needs written approval from the finance controller.

## 6. Guests

### 6.1 Guest travel
Guests are not allowed on employee shuttles or cabs. A guest of a visiting client may be transported only with written approval from a vice president.

## 7. No-show and penalties

### 7.1 No-show penalty
The first no-show in a calendar month results in a warning. From the second no-show in the same month, a fee of Rs 200 is charged for each no-show.

### 7.2 Repeated no-shows
After 5 no-shows in a calendar month, transport is suspended for the rest of that month.

## 8. Purpose, scope and definitions

### 8.1 Purpose of this policy
The purpose of this policy is to establish a consistent framework for the provision, management and oversight of corporate transport services, ensuring that all travel arrangements align with the organization's operational objectives, safety standards and fiscal responsibilities. It delineates the responsibilities of the reporting manager, department head, finance controller, transport desk, security escort and vice president, thereby creating clear lines of accountability and facilitating coordinated decision-making across functional areas. By referencing the applicable provisions in sections one point one, one point two, two point one, two point two, three point one, three point two, three point three, four point one, four point two, five point one, five point two, five point three, six point one, seven point one and seven point two, the policy integrates existing eligibility criteria, timing constraints, security requirements, cost controls and compliance mechanisms into a unified approach.

Through this consolidated guidance, the organization seeks to promote efficient use of resources, uphold the safety and security of personnel, and support the seamless execution of business activities. The policy further aims to provide transparent expectations for employees and contractors, thereby reducing ambiguity and fostering a culture of responsible travel conduct throughout the enterprise.

### 8.2 Scope and applicability
The purpose of this policy is to establish a consistent framework for the provision of corporate transport services across all operational locations, ensuring that eligibility, scheduling, security, and financial controls are applied uniformly. All employees, contractors, and approved guests are subject to the criteria set out in the relevant sections, including the grade and distance considerations of section one point one, the shift-based eligibility described in section one point two, and the timing parameters defined in section two point one. The policy further delineates the responsibilities of reporting managers, department heads, finance controllers, the transport desk, security escorts, and the vice president in overseeing compliance with the cut-off timings, cancellation procedures, and security escort requirements as referenced in sections two point two, three point one, and three point two.

Applicability extends to routine commuting, ad-hoc trips, weekend and public-holiday travel, as well as airport transfers, each governed by the specific provisions of sections four point one, four point two, five point one, and five point two. Financial limits, guest travel protocols, and penalties for no-shows are enforced in accordance with sections five point three, six point one, seven point one, and seven point two, ensuring that all transport activities align with corporate objectives and risk-management standards.

### 8.3 Definitions of common terms
The purpose of this section is to establish a shared vocabulary that underpins all transport-related processes throughout the organization. "Employee" refers to any individual who is formally engaged by the company and is eligible for transport services as defined in the applicable eligibility sections. "Authorized travel" denotes any movement that conforms to the criteria set out in the relevant eligibility and cut-off provisions, and which has received the requisite approval in accordance with the ad-hoc trip approval process. "Transport request" is the formal submission made by an employee to the transport desk, which must contain sufficient detail to allow verification against the grade and distance parameters and to trigger any required security escort as stipulated in the security escort section.

"Security escort" describes the accompaniment of an employee by a designated security representative when travel occurs under conditions that invoke the escort requirement. "Cost cap" identifies the maximum allowable expense for a single journey, as prescribed in the cost cap provision. "No-show" refers to the failure to board a scheduled vehicle without prior cancellation, invoking the penalties outlined in the no-show penalty sections. These definitions provide the foundation for consistent interpretation and application of the transport policy.

## 9. Roles and responsibilities

### 9.1 Responsibilities of employees
Employees are required to coordinate with their reporting manager to confirm eligibility for transport services in accordance with the criteria set out in the grade and distance provision and the shift-based eligibility provision. The department head must ensure that any request for transport aligns with the applicable cut-off timings and that any cancellation follows the established cancellation protocol. Finance control must verify that each approved trip complies with the cost cap and that any ad-hoc travel receives the necessary approval before scheduling.

The transport desk is responsible for arranging the appropriate vehicle, securing a security escort when required, and confirming that night-drop timing constraints are observed. Security escort personnel must accompany designated journeys and report any safety concerns as stipulated in the safety reporting requirement. The vice president holds ultimate accountability for ensuring that weekend and public-holiday transport provisions are applied consistently and that guest travel adheres to the guest travel guidelines. All employees must understand that failure to appear for a scheduled service will trigger the no-show penalty and that repeated infractions will be addressed under the repeated no-show provisions.

### 9.2 Responsibilities of reporting managers
The reporting manager shall ensure that each employee's eligibility for transport services is verified in accordance with the criteria set out in section one point one and section one point two, confirming that the appropriate grade and distance thresholds are met and that shift patterns align with the established eligibility framework. Prior to any scheduled departure, the manager must confirm that the applicable cut-off timings have been observed as described in section two point one, and must coordinate any required cancellations through the transport desk in line with the procedures outlined in section two point two.

In addition, the reporting manager is responsible for liaising with the security escort to guarantee that all night-time drop requirements are satisfied as per section three point two, and for ensuring that any safety concerns are promptly recorded following the protocol in section three point three. The manager must also approve ad-hoc travel requests in accordance with section five point one, verify that airport travel complies with section five point two, and confirm that all cost considerations remain within the limit set in section five point three.

### 9.3 Responsibilities of the transport desk
The transport desk shall serve as the central coordination hub for all employee travel requests, ensuring that each submission is logged, verified, and routed in accordance with the applicable provisions set out in the relevant sections. Upon receipt of a request, the desk must confirm eligibility against the criteria described in section one point one and section one point two, and then forward the request to the appropriate reporting manager or department head for endorsement. All approvals must be recorded and any deviations from the standard schedule shall be communicated promptly to the finance controller, referencing the cut-off parameters of section two point one and the cancellation guidelines of section two point two.

In addition, the transport desk is responsible for arranging security escort services where required, as mandated in section three point one, and for scheduling night-time drop-offs in line with the timing restrictions of section three point two. The desk must also maintain a register of all ad-hoc trips, ensuring that each is authorized in accordance with section five point one and that any cost limitations outlined in section five point three are observed. Finally, the desk shall monitor compliance with guest travel protocols of section six point one and enforce the no-show penalties described in sections seven point one and seven point two, escalating repeated infractions to the vice president for further action.

### 9.4 Responsibilities of HR and administration
The Human Resources function, in partnership with the administration team, is charged with overseeing the eligibility verification process for all transport participants, ensuring that the criteria set out in the applicable sections are applied consistently across the organization. HR shall maintain up-to-date records of employee classifications and shift patterns, and shall coordinate with the reporting manager and department head to confirm that each request aligns with the parameters described in the relevant eligibility and cut-off provisions. In addition, HR is responsible for communicating any changes to the transport policy, distributing updates to all staff, and providing guidance on the documentation required for ad-hoc trip approval as referenced in the appropriate section.

Administration, through the transport desk, shall manage the scheduling and allocation of vehicles, enforce the cancellation and no-show procedures, and ensure that all bookings comply with the weekend, public holiday, and night-drop timing rules. The finance controller will be consulted to verify that each arrangement respects the cost limitation outlined in the cost cap provision, while the security escort and vice president will be notified of any exceptions that involve security considerations or executive travel. Together, HR and administration must monitor compliance, address any discrepancies promptly, and report safety incidents in accordance with the designated safety reporting requirement.

## 10. Rosters and route management

### 10.1 Roster preparation
The transport desk shall compile the weekly roster in accordance with the eligibility criteria set out in section one point one and section one point two, ensuring that each assignment aligns with the applicable cut-off timings described in section two point one. All entries must be cross-checked against the cancellation provisions of section two point two and the weekend and public holiday parameters of sections four point one and four point two, respectively, to guarantee compliance with the organization's operational constraints.

Prior to finalisation, the draft roster shall be reviewed by the reporting manager and the department head to confirm that any required security escort, as stipulated in section three point one, and night-drop considerations from section three point two are properly incorporated. The finance controller must verify that each scheduled trip adheres to the cost limitations outlined in section five point three, while the vice president provides final approval for any ad-hoc or airport travel that falls under sections five point one and five point two. Once approved, the transport desk will distribute the roster to all relevant parties, and any deviations must be reported in accordance with the safety reporting requirements of section three point three.

### 10.2 Route planning principles
The transport desk shall develop each roster in accordance with the overarching principles of efficiency, safety and compliance, ensuring that every itinerary aligns with the eligibility criteria set out in section one point one and the shift-based considerations described in section one point two. Routes shall be optimized to respect the cut-off timings identified in section two point one, while also providing sufficient buffer for any adjustments required under the cancellation provisions of section two point two. All planned journeys must incorporate the security escort requirements of section three point one and observe the night-drop timing constraints of section three point two, thereby guaranteeing that personnel are conveyed within the parameters established for secure and timely delivery.

In addition, the planning process shall account for weekend and public-holiday service specifications outlined in sections four point one and four point two, and shall integrate the approval workflow for ad-hoc travel as defined in section five point one. Cost considerations shall remain within the limit set in section five point three, and any guest travel shall be arranged in strict accordance with section six point one. Compliance monitoring and incident reporting shall follow the safety reporting protocol of section three point three, ensuring continuous improvement of route management practices.

### 10.3 Pick-up and drop points
The selection of pick-up and drop points shall be governed by the operational framework set out in the Rosters and Route Management chapter, with particular reference to the eligibility criteria defined in section one point one and the shift-based considerations of section one point two. All designated locations must be approved by the transport desk and communicated to the reporting manager prior to the commencement of each scheduling cycle. The transport desk shall ensure that each point complies with the security escort requirements of section three point one and aligns with the night-drop timing constraints of section three point two, thereby guaranteeing that all movements occur within the permissible windows established in section two point one.

Any modification to an established point, whether due to construction, security concerns, or operational changes, must be submitted for approval in accordance with the ad-hoc trip protocol of section five point one and documented in the safety reporting system of section three point three. The finance controller shall verify that any additional cost incurred as a result of a change does not exceed the limit set in section five point three. Failure to adhere to these procedures will be treated as a breach of the no-show policy outlined in sections seven point one and seven point two, and appropriate corrective action will be taken.

### 10.4 Handling shift changes
When a shift alteration is communicated, the reporting manager must promptly inform the transport desk, ensuring that the revised departure and return windows align with the cut-off timings outlined in section 2.1. The transport desk shall verify that the adjusted itinerary continues to satisfy the eligibility criteria described in section 1.2 and that any required security escort, as stipulated in section 3.1, remains available for the new schedule. Should the change affect a night drop, the desk must also confirm compliance with the parameters set out in section 3.2, and record the modification in the central roster system for audit purposes.

Any cancellation that results from the shift change must be processed in accordance with the procedures in section 2.2, and any associated cost implications shall be evaluated against the limit set in section 5.3. The department head and finance controller are responsible for reviewing the financial impact of the amendment, while the vice president may be consulted for exceptions that fall outside routine parameters. All adjustments shall be documented and reported as required by the safety reporting obligations in section 3.3.

## 11. Vehicles and drivers

### 11.1 Vehicle standards and inspection
The vehicle standards and inspection protocol shall be applied uniformly to all transport assets utilized for official travel, ensuring that each vehicle meets the safety, comfort, and environmental criteria established by corporate governance. All vehicles must undergo a comprehensive pre-departure inspection that includes verification of mechanical integrity, functional safety equipment, and compliance with the emissions thresholds defined in the applicable environmental policy. The transport desk shall maintain a current register of inspected vehicles and shall coordinate with the security escort function to confirm that any special security requirements, as outlined in section three point one, are satisfied before vehicle release.

Subsequent to each trip, the driver is required to complete a post-journey inspection checklist, documenting any anomalies and forwarding the report to the finance controller for cost reconciliation in accordance with the limit set in section five point three. Any deviation from the prescribed standards shall trigger an immediate remedial action plan, overseen by the department head, and shall be recorded in the safety reporting system referenced in section three point three. Ongoing compliance will be monitored through periodic audits conducted by the vice president's office, with findings reported to the reporting manager for corrective follow-up.

### 11.2 Driver verification and onboarding
The transport function shall ensure that every individual designated to operate a vehicle for the organization undergoes a comprehensive verification process prior to commencement of duties. All prospective drivers must present valid licensure, satisfactory background clearance, and evidence of recent medical fitness, as stipulated in the relevant regulatory framework. The verification sequence shall be coordinated by the transport desk in conjunction with the security escort, and the final approval shall be recorded in the system referenced in section five point one, thereby guaranteeing alignment with the organization's risk management standards.

Upon successful completion of the verification stage, the onboarding protocol requires the driver to attend a mandatory orientation session that covers safety expectations, reporting obligations, and the procedural guidelines outlined in section three point three. The orientation shall be signed off by the reporting manager and the finance controller to confirm awareness of cost considerations as defined in section five point three. Ongoing compliance will be monitored through periodic reviews, and any deviation from the established criteria shall trigger remedial action in accordance with the provisions set out in section seven point one and section seven point two.

### 11.3 Driver conduct and training
The organization expects every driver to embody professionalism, reliability and safety in all interactions, whether transporting staff, guests or external partners. Drivers must complete the mandatory orientation program before assuming any assignment, and they are required to attend periodic refresher sessions that reinforce the standards set out in the core policy. All training modules are coordinated through the transport desk and must be documented in the driver's file, with verification of completion reviewed by the finance controller and the reporting manager. Any deviation from the prescribed conduct, including failure to adhere to the security escort requirements of section three point one or the night-drop timing constraints of section three point two, shall be reported immediately to the department head for corrective action.

In addition, drivers are obliged to maintain a clean driving record and to report any incident, near-miss or safety concern in accordance with the procedures described in section three point three. The transport desk will monitor compliance and may impose the no-show penalties referenced in sections seven point one and seven point two for repeated infractions. Continuous improvement is encouraged, and drivers are invited to suggest enhancements to the training curriculum through the established feedback channel managed by the vice president.

### 11.4 Vendor management and performance review
The transport function shall maintain a structured approach to vendor oversight, ensuring that all service providers align with the organization's standards for reliability, safety and cost efficiency. The transport desk, in coordination with the finance controller, shall conduct periodic performance assessments that reference the cost limitations described in section five point three and the eligibility criteria outlined in sections one point one and one point two. Each review shall evaluate adherence to the timing parameters set out in sections two point one and three point two, as well as compliance with the security escort requirements of section three point one and the safety reporting obligations of section three point three. Findings shall be documented and presented to the vice president for final approval, with any deviations escalated to the reporting manager and department head for corrective action.

When a vendor fails to meet the established benchmarks, the transport desk shall initiate a remediation process that incorporates the cancellation provisions of section two point two and the no-show penalties of sections seven point one and seven point two. Persistent non-compliance may result in termination of the contract, subject to the approval hierarchy defined in the policy, and the selection of an alternative provider shall follow the ad-hoc trip approval framework of section five point one. This disciplined methodology ensures that vehicle and driver services remain consistent with corporate expectations while supporting operational continuity.

## 12. Trip tracking and technology

### 12.1 Live trip tracking
The live-trip tracking function is a mandatory component of every authorized journey and must be activated at the moment the vehicle departs the designated pick-up point. The system records real-time location data, which is continuously streamed to the transport desk for monitoring and to the reporting manager for oversight. Any deviation from the planned route or unexpected delay must be flagged immediately through the provided interface, allowing the department head to assess the situation and, where necessary, to engage a security escort as outlined in the relevant security provision. The transport desk is responsible for ensuring that the tracking feed remains active until the vehicle reaches the final drop-off location, in accordance with the night-drop timing guidance.

All recorded data are retained in the central repository for a period prescribed by the corporate records policy and are subject to review during any safety reporting process. The finance controller may reference the captured mileage and duration metrics when evaluating compliance with the cost cap established in the appropriate financial section. Failure to maintain an uninterrupted tracking signal will be treated as a breach of the no-show penalty framework and may trigger the remedial actions described in the repeated no-show provision.

### 12.2 Use of the booking app
The booking application shall serve as the sole mechanism for initiating, modifying, and concluding all authorized journeys, and every employee must access it through the corporate portal using their assigned credentials. Requests must be entered in accordance with the eligibility criteria outlined in section one point one and section one point two, and any alteration after the cut-off defined in section two point one shall be processed only through the formal cancellation procedure described in section two point two. The transport desk will receive each submission and, where required, will coordinate with the security escort provisions set out in section three point one, as well as observe the night-drop parameters of section three point two, ensuring that all safety concerns are logged pursuant to section three point three.

All approvals for non-routine travel shall reference the ad-hoc trip protocol of section five point one, and any airport-related itinerary must comply with the stipulations of section five point two while remaining within the financial limitation described in section five point three. Failure to adhere to the booking workflow will trigger the no-show consequences detailed in sections seven point one and seven point two, and any guest travel must be processed in line with section six point one.

### 12.3 Data privacy and retention of trip data
The transport function shall capture all trip-related information in a secure system that complies with applicable data-protection legislation and internal confidentiality standards. Access to the repository is limited to the reporting manager, department head, finance controller, transport desk and vice president, each of whom may retrieve records solely for legitimate operational, audit or compliance purposes as set out in section five point one and section five point three. All entries shall be timestamped automatically by the system and retained for the period prescribed by corporate records policy, after which they will be archived or destroyed in accordance with the retention schedule without further manual intervention.

Any modification, export or deletion of trip data must be logged and reviewed by the security escort function to ensure integrity, as required by section three point three. The system shall encrypt data at rest and in transit, and shall enforce role-based controls that prevent unauthorized viewing or alteration. Should a request for personal data arise, the transport desk will coordinate the response in line with the procedures outlined in section two point two, ensuring that the privacy rights of employees and guests are respected while maintaining the accuracy of the transport record.

## 13. Safety and security procedures

### 13.1 How the security escort is deployed
The deployment of a security escort shall be coordinated through the transport desk in conjunction with the reporting manager and the department head, ensuring that each request aligns with the parameters established in the relevant safety and security sections. When a request is received, the transport desk will verify eligibility against the criteria outlined in the grade and distance provisions and the shift-based eligibility guidelines, then schedule an escort in accordance with the cut-off timings and night-drop requirements. The escort will accompany the employee from the designated pick-up point to the final destination, maintaining continuous presence until the journey is completed, and will document any incidents as required by the safety reporting procedures.

Any deviation from the standard escort protocol, including cancellations or alterations, must be processed through the finance controller and approved in line with the ad-hoc trip approval process. The escort's involvement shall be recorded for audit purposes, and any breach of the no-show or repeated no-show policies will trigger the applicable penalties as described in the relevant sections. This approach ensures consistent protection while adhering to the established cost cap and overall safety framework.

### 13.2 Emergency helpline and panic alerts
The Emergency Helpline and Panic Alerts protocol is established to ensure immediate assistance for any employee who encounters a threat or unsafe condition while using corporate transport services. All personnel must be aware that activation of the panic alert, whether through the vehicle's dedicated button or the mobile application, will automatically notify the transport desk, the designated security escort, and the reporting manager. The response sequence follows the procedures outlined in section three point one and incorporates the escalation steps described in section three point three, guaranteeing that a qualified escort is dispatched without delay and that a formal safety report is generated for subsequent review.

In addition, the emergency helpline operates continuously and is staffed by trained operators who will verify the incident, coordinate with the finance controller for any immediate expense authorizations, and inform the vice president of any significant security breach. Employees are required to familiarize themselves with the contact numbers posted in each vehicle and to test the alert mechanism during routine safety briefings, as stipulated in section two point one and section two point two. This ensures that the organization maintains a robust and responsive safety framework for all transport activities.

### 13.3 Incident investigation process
The investigation of any incident that involves corporate transport shall commence immediately upon receipt of a report, with the reporting manager responsible for initiating the formal record and notifying the transport desk. The department head shall ensure that all relevant documentation, including the security escort log and safety reporting entries, is preserved in accordance with the provisions set out in the applicable sections. The finance controller will be consulted to verify any cost implications against the limit set in the relevant cost cap provision, while the vice president will be kept informed of the progress and any potential impact on broader operations.

A security escort shall be assigned to secure the scene and to gather witness statements, adhering to the requirements described in the security escort clause. The transport desk shall coordinate with the appropriate internal teams to secure any necessary evidence and to arrange for a timely debrief. Upon completion of the fact-finding phase, a comprehensive report shall be compiled and reviewed by the department head, with any corrective actions documented and communicated to all stakeholders. The final findings shall be filed in accordance with the procedures outlined in the safety reporting and no-show penalty sections, ensuring full compliance with corporate governance standards.

### 13.4 Safety review committee
The Safety Review Committee shall be convened under the authority of the chapter on safety and security procedures and shall operate as the principal body for evaluating all incidents and potential hazards associated with corporate transport. Membership shall include the reporting manager, the department head, the finance controller, the transport desk representative, the security escort, and the vice president, each bringing the perspective required to assess compliance with the applicable provisions set out in sections three point one, three point three, and seven point one. The committee shall meet regularly to examine reports generated in accordance with section three point three and to recommend corrective actions that align with the cost cap described in section five point three, ensuring that any remedial measures remain financially responsible.

All findings and recommendations shall be documented and communicated to the relevant operational units within a timeframe consistent with the cut-off timings identified in section two point one. The committee shall also review any repeated no-show occurrences as referenced in section seven point two, and shall propose policy adjustments to mitigate future risk. By maintaining a structured review process, the committee supports a culture of safety, accountability, and continuous improvement across all transport activities.

## 14. Health, accessibility and special needs

### 14.1 Medical emergencies during a trip
When a medical emergency arises while an employee is in transit, the immediate priority is the health and safety of the individual, and the response must be coordinated through the transport desk in conjunction with the designated security escort. The transport desk shall be notified without delay, and the escort shall follow the procedures outlined in section three point one to secure appropriate medical assistance. If the incident occurs during a period covered by the night drop provisions, the timing requirements of section three point two shall be observed to ensure that any deviation from the scheduled drop does not compromise the emergency response.

All relevant details of the incident, including the nature of the emergency, actions taken, and any assistance rendered, must be documented in accordance with the safety reporting obligations set out in section three point three. The reporting manager and department head shall review the report to confirm compliance with the applicable cut-off timings of section two point one and to determine whether any cancellation protocols from section two point two are triggered. The finance controller will verify that any incurred costs remain within the limit set in section five point three, while the vice president will be apprised of any systemic issues that may require policy revision.

### 14.2 Support for employees with disabilities
The organization recognises that employees with disabilities may require adjustments to standard transport provisions, and it commits to delivering equitable access in accordance with applicable legislation and internal standards. All requests for accommodation shall be directed to the reporting manager, who will coordinate with the department head and the transport desk to evaluate the need and identify suitable modifications, such as vehicle adaptations, assistance from a security escort, or alternative scheduling, as set out in section three point one and section three point three. The finance controller will ensure that any additional costs remain within the limit set in section five point three, while the vice president will oversee compliance and resource allocation.

When a disability-related request intersects with existing eligibility criteria, shift-based eligibility and cut-off timings shall be applied as described in sections one point two and two point one, without prejudice to the employee's entitlement. Should a scheduled service be altered or cancelled due to a disability accommodation, the procedures in section two point two shall be followed, and any impact on weekend transport or public holidays shall be managed in line with sections four point one and four point two. The policy aims to foster an inclusive environment while maintaining operational integrity.

### 14.3 Support during pregnancy and recovery
The organization recognises that employees who are expecting a child or recovering from childbirth may require adjustments to their transport arrangements in order to maintain safety, comfort and continuity of work responsibilities. The reporting manager, in consultation with the department head and the finance controller, shall evaluate each request and coordinate with the transport desk to provide a vehicle that meets the specific mobility and ergonomic needs identified. Any modification to the standard schedule shall be aligned with the applicable cut-off timings as set out in section two point one, and any deviation from the usual route or vehicle type shall be documented in accordance with the safety reporting requirements of section three point three.

When travel occurs outside of regular operating hours, the provision of a security escort shall be arranged as described in section three point one, and any night-time drop shall comply with the parameters established in section three point two. All approvals for ad-hoc adjustments shall follow the process outlined in section five point one, and any associated costs shall remain within the limit set in section five point three. This approach ensures that employees receive consistent, respectful support throughout pregnancy and the recovery period while adhering to the broader transport policy framework.

## 15. Disruptions and contingency planning

### 15.1 Weather and flooding
The organization shall treat adverse weather and flooding as a distinct category of disruption, invoking the contingency framework outlined in the chapter on disruptions and contingency planning. When conditions meet the criteria defined in the relevant operational guidelines, the transport desk shall coordinate with the reporting manager and the department head to assess the impact on scheduled movements, referencing the cut-off timings established in section two point one. Any deviation from the normal schedule shall be communicated promptly to all affected parties, and any required adjustments shall be processed in accordance with the cancellation provisions set out in section two point two.

In circumstances where flooding imposes a safety risk, the security escort requirements of section three point one shall be reviewed, and the night drop timings of section three point two shall be re-evaluated to ensure compliance with safety reporting obligations in section three point three. Should an ad-hoc trip become necessary due to the disruption, approval must follow the procedure described in section five point one, while any associated costs shall remain subject to the limit set in section five point three. All actions shall be documented to support subsequent analysis and continuous improvement of the organization's resilience to weather-related events.

### 15.2 Strikes, curfews and public disturbances
The organization acknowledges that strikes, curfews and public disturbances may impede scheduled travel and therefore requires all employees to consult the transport desk promptly when such events are announced. The transport desk, in coordination with the reporting manager and the department head, shall assess the impact on the applicable cut-off timings as set out in section two point one and determine whether alternative arrangements are feasible. When a disruption is identified, the finance controller must verify that any deviation from the cost cap described in section five point three remains within permissible limits, and the vice president will be informed of any significant operational impact.

In circumstances where travel must be postponed or cancelled due to a curfew or civil unrest, the cancellation protocol referenced in section two point two shall be observed, and the security escort requirements of section three point one shall be re-evaluated to ensure employee safety. Should a night drop be required outside the normal window, the provisions of section three point two shall apply, and any safety incident must be reported in accordance with section three point three. All actions taken shall be documented for audit and future contingency planning.

### 15.3 Vehicle breakdown during a trip
When a vehicle experiences a mechanical failure or any other form of breakdown while a trip is in progress, the driver must immediately notify the transport desk and the reporting manager, providing a concise description of the incident and the estimated time required to reach a safe location. The transport desk shall coordinate with the security escort, if applicable, to ensure the safety of passengers and to arrange a prompt replacement vehicle in accordance with the procedures outlined in the relevant sections. The department head shall be informed of any disruption that may affect scheduled arrivals, and the finance controller shall be alerted to record any additional costs that may arise, referencing the cost cap established in the appropriate clause.

The replacement vehicle shall be dispatched without undue delay, adhering to the cut-off timings specified in the applicable section, and any deviation from the planned itinerary shall be documented for later review. Passengers are required to complete the safety reporting form as described in the designated safety reporting provision, and any cancellation of subsequent legs of the journey must follow the cancellation guidelines set forth in the corresponding section.

### 15.4 Business continuity for the transport service
The transport service shall remain operational under all circumstances that could affect normal business functions, and any interruption must be addressed through the established escalation hierarchy. When a disruption is identified, the reporting manager must immediately notify the transport desk, which in turn will coordinate with the department head and the finance controller to assess the impact on scheduled movements. The vice president will be informed of any event that could extend beyond the applicable cut-off defined in the relevant sections, and a contingency plan will be activated in accordance with the provisions set out in section one point one and section one point two, ensuring that eligibility criteria and grade considerations continue to be respected.

In the event of a prolonged outage, the transport desk shall arrange alternative arrangements that comply with the security escort requirements of section three point one and the night drop timing constraints of section three point two. All deviations from the standard schedule must be documented and reported through the safety reporting mechanism described in section three point three, and any associated costs shall not exceed the limit set in section five point three. Guest travel, weekend service, and public holiday provisions shall be observed as outlined in sections six point one, four point one, and four point two, respectively, to maintain continuity and uphold the organization's commitment to reliable employee mobility.

## 16. Conduct, discipline and grievances

### 16.1 Employee conduct in vehicles
All employees who are transported under the corporate program are expected to observe a standard of conduct that reflects the organization's commitment to safety, professionalism, and respect for fellow travelers. While seated, occupants must remain seated unless a legitimate need to move arises, and all personal devices should be used in a manner that does not distract the driver or compromise vehicle security, as outlined in the provisions of section three point one and section three point three. The reporting manager and department head share responsibility for ensuring that their team members understand these expectations, and any deviation shall be reported promptly to the transport desk for appropriate follow-up in accordance with the disciplinary framework set out in section seven point one and section seven point two.

Furthermore, the finance controller and vice president are tasked with overseeing compliance monitoring and ensuring that any breach does not result in undue cost or liability, consistent with the cost considerations described in section five point three. Security escorts shall enforce the conduct standards during night drops and weekend operations, referencing the relevant timing and eligibility criteria in sections two point one, two point two, four point one, and four point two. Failure to adhere to these expectations may trigger the penalties and remedial actions detailed in the grievance procedures of the conduct chapter.

### 16.2 Disciplinary process for violations
The disciplinary process for violations is designed to ensure consistent and fair treatment of all employees while safeguarding the integrity of the transport program. When a breach is identified, the reporting manager must document the incident and forward the report to the department head, who will evaluate the facts against the applicable provisions set out in the relevant sections, such as the eligibility criteria in section 1.2, the cut-off requirements in section 2.1, and the security escort obligations in section 3.1. The finance controller will verify any cost-related implications in accordance with section 5.3, and the transport desk will coordinate any necessary corrective actions, including the scheduling of remedial training or the imposition of the no-show penalty described in section 7.1.

If the initial review determines that disciplinary action is warranted, the vice president, in consultation with the department head and security escort, will determine the appropriate level of response, ranging from a formal warning to more severe measures as outlined in the grievance procedures. The employee will be afforded an opportunity to present their perspective, and a written record of the decision will be retained for future reference, ensuring transparency and compliance with the overarching conduct standards.

### 16.3 Grievance redressal
The grievance redressal process is overseen by the reporting manager in coordination with the department head and the vice president, ensuring that any concerns related to transport services are addressed promptly and impartially. Employees who wish to raise a grievance must submit a written statement to the transport desk, which will log the issue and forward it to the appropriate authority within the applicable time frame defined in section two point one. The transport desk shall acknowledge receipt of the grievance and initiate an investigation, consulting the security escort where relevant and observing the reporting obligations set out in section three point three.

All parties involved are required to cooperate fully with the investigation, and the finance controller will review any cost implications in accordance with section five point three. Upon completion of the review, a decision will be communicated to the employee, and any corrective actions will be implemented as prescribed in the relevant sections. Should the employee remain dissatisfied, the matter may be escalated to the vice president for final adjudication, with the outcome recorded in accordance with the established documentation standards.

### 16.4 Escalation matrix
The escalation matrix for transport-related concerns is designed to ensure timely resolution while preserving the integrity of the service. An employee who encounters a breach of the standards set out in the relevant sections should first notify the reporting manager, who will assess the issue against the criteria established in section 3.1 and section 3.3. If the matter is not resolved at that level, the department head is engaged to review the circumstances and determine whether further action is required, referencing the provisions of section 2.2 and section 7.1 as appropriate. Should the department head deem the issue escalated, the finance controller is consulted to verify any financial implications in line with section 5.3, and the transport desk is instructed to implement corrective measures.

In cases where the initial escalation does not achieve satisfactory remediation, the vice president assumes responsibility for final adjudication, ensuring alignment with the overarching policies of the conduct, discipline and grievances chapter. Throughout the process, all communications must be documented and forwarded to the security escort team when safety considerations arise, as mandated by section 3.1, to guarantee that any risk is promptly mitigated and recorded.

## 17. Cost management and audit

### 17.1 Billing and reconciliation with vendors
The finance controller shall oversee all vendor invoicing related to transport services, ensuring that each submission aligns with the cost cap established in section five point three and conforms to the approval workflow described in section five point one. Upon receipt of an invoice, the transport desk is responsible for verifying that the charges correspond to the authorized trip parameters and that any applicable adjustments for cancellations, as outlined in section two point two, have been correctly applied. Discrepancies must be escalated to the reporting manager for resolution before any payment is authorized.

Reconciliation of vendor statements shall be performed on a regular cycle, with the finance controller confirming that all recorded expenses match the approved allocations and that any guest travel, as referenced in section six point one, is accurately reflected. Any variance beyond the permissible threshold shall trigger a review by the department head, and corrective action shall be documented in accordance with the audit requirements of the cost management chapter. All reconciled records shall be retained for the period mandated by corporate policy and made available for inspection upon request.

### 17.2 Cost allocation to departments
The allocation of transport expenses to the appropriate departmental budgets shall be performed in accordance with the principles set out in the cost management and audit chapter. Each department shall receive a charge reflecting the actual usage of transport services, as recorded by the transport desk and verified against the approval records maintained by the finance controller. The methodology for determining the appropriate share shall follow the guidance established in the relevant cost cap provision and shall be applied consistently across all reporting periods.

Responsibility for the accurate distribution of costs rests with the reporting manager, who must ensure that the data supplied by the transport desk aligns with the eligibility criteria defined in the grade and distance and shift-based eligibility provisions. Any adjustments required due to cancellations, weekend or public-holiday usage, or ad-hoc trip approvals shall be documented in accordance with the established audit trail requirements. The department head shall review the allocated charges and confirm their correctness before final submission to the finance controller for inclusion in the departmental financial statements.

### 17.3 Internal audit and compliance review
The internal audit function shall be coordinated by the finance controller in partnership with the transport desk, ensuring that every expense claim and trip record is examined against the cost parameters established in the relevant sections. All transport transactions will be cross-checked for compliance with the applicable cut-off timings, cancellation protocols, weekend and public-holiday provisions, and the cost cap, as set out in sections two point one, two point two, four point one, four point two and five point three respectively. Any deviation identified during the review shall be documented and escalated to the reporting manager and the vice president for corrective action, with a summary report submitted to the department head on a quarterly basis.

The audit schedule shall incorporate a random sampling of ad-hoc approvals, airport trips and guest travel entries, referencing sections five point one, five point two and six point one to verify that approvals were obtained in accordance with policy. Findings related to safety reporting, security escort requirements and night-drop timing shall be aligned with sections three point one, three point two and three point three, and any breach shall trigger the no-show penalty process as described in sections seven point one and seven point two.

## 18. Governance and administration

### 18.1 Policy ownership
The governance of the transport programme rests with the senior leadership team, which delegates specific responsibilities to ensure consistent application and continuous improvement. The vice president assumes overall accountability for policy compliance, while the finance controller oversees budgetary adherence and monitors the cost cap referenced in section five point three. The transport desk is charged with day-to-day administration, including the processing of ad-hoc trip approvals as set out in section five point one and the coordination of airport journeys in accordance with section five point two. All changes to eligibility criteria, such as those described in sections one point one and one point two, must be reviewed by the department head and ratified by the reporting manager before implementation.

Security considerations are managed by the security escort function, which ensures that the requirements of section three point one are met for each scheduled movement, and that night-drop timing constraints in section three point two are observed. Any incidents or safety concerns must be recorded following the procedure in section three point three, and the compiled data shall be reported to the vice president for periodic review. The policy owner is responsible for maintaining the document, communicating updates to all stakeholders, and confirming that the weekend and public-holiday provisions in sections four point one and four point two remain aligned with operational needs.

### 18.2 Review and amendment process
The governance framework requires that any alteration to the transport policy be initiated through a formal request submitted by the reporting manager or department head to the transport desk, with endorsement from the finance controller. The request must cite the specific provision under review, reference the applicable clause by its alphanumeric identifier, and include a rationale that aligns with operational efficiency, fiscal responsibility, or regulatory compliance. Upon receipt, the transport desk compiles the proposal and forwards it to the vice president for preliminary assessment, after which a cross-functional review panel, comprising representatives from security, finance, and human resources, convenes to evaluate the impact and consistency with the existing governance structure.

Following deliberation, the panel drafts an amendment memorandum that outlines the proposed change, the supporting evidence, and any required updates to related sections such as the grade and distance criteria, shift-based eligibility, cut-off timings, cancellation procedures, security escort requirements, night drop timings, safety reporting obligations, weekend and public-holiday provisions, ad-hoc trip approval, airport travel guidelines, cost caps, guest travel rules, and no-show penalties. The memorandum is then submitted to the vice president for final approval; once authorized, the revised policy is communicated to all stakeholders and entered into the official repository, where it supersedes prior language and becomes effective as stipulated in the amendment record.

### 18.3 Handling exceptions to this policy
The governance framework requires that any deviation from the established transport provisions be documented and approved through the formal exception process. When a circumstance arises that falls outside the parameters set out in the relevant sections, the requesting party must submit a written justification to the transport desk, citing the specific clause that would otherwise apply, such as the grade and distance criteria, shift-based eligibility, or weekend and public holiday provisions. The transport desk, in coordination with the reporting manager and the finance controller, will evaluate the request against the security escort requirements, night-drop timing constraints, and the cost cap limitation, ensuring that the proposed exception does not compromise safety, budgetary controls, or operational continuity.

Upon approval, the exception record shall be entered into the central log and communicated to the department head and the vice president for oversight. The security escort and safety reporting obligations remain in effect for the duration of the exception, and any cancellation or no-show must adhere to the established cancellation and penalty provisions. All approved exceptions are subject to periodic review to confirm alignment with the overarching policy objectives.

### 18.4 Related documents and forms
The governance framework for transport operations is supported by a suite of ancillary documents and standardized forms that must be completed, filed, and retained in accordance with the procedures outlined in the relevant sections. All request and approval forms reference the applicable cut-off timings set out in section two point one and must be routed through the transport desk for verification before any scheduling action is taken, as required by section five point one. The security escort protocol described in section three point one is accompanied by a dedicated escort checklist, while the night-drop schedule referenced in section three point two is documented on the nightly operations sheet, both of which are to be signed by the reporting manager and retained for audit purposes.

Financial reconciliation of transport expenses utilizes the cost-cap template identified in section five point three and must be reviewed by the finance controller prior to final submission. Guest travel arrangements are captured on the guest travel request form, which aligns with the provisions of section six point one, and any cancellation or no-show incident is recorded on the incident log prescribed in sections seven point one and seven point two. All forms are stored in the central repository and made available for periodic review by the vice president and department heads.

### 18.5 Contact points
The governance framework designates specific points of contact to ensure consistent administration of transport services across all locations. All requests for routine scheduling, modifications, or inquiries shall be directed to the transport desk, which acts as the central hub for coordination and records all interactions in accordance with the procedures outlined in section five point one. Escalations that involve eligibility verification, cost considerations, or exceptional circumstances must be routed through the reporting manager, who collaborates with the department head and finance controller to confirm compliance with the applicable cut-off and cost cap provisions referenced in sections two point one and five point three. For matters requiring security oversight, such as escort arrangements or night-time drop coordination, the appropriate liaison is the security escort, whose responsibilities are defined in sections three point one and three point two, and any safety incident must be reported following the protocol set out in section three point three. Finally, the vice president retains ultimate authority for policy exceptions and may be consulted when strategic decisions intersect with transport administration, ensuring alignment with the overarching corporate objectives.
