# Borealis Systems - Commute and Transport Policy
Version: 1
Effective: 2026-03-01

## 1. Eligibility

### 1.1 Distance-based eligibility
All permanent employees living within 25 km of the office are eligible for transport, regardless of grade. Contract staff are eligible only if their manager confirms it in writing.

### 1.2 Shift-based eligibility
Employees on the night shift (10:00 PM to 6:00 AM) are always eligible for drop and pickup.

## 2. Booking

### 2.1 Cut-off timings
Transport must be booked at least 2 hours before the pickup or drop time. There is no ad-hoc approval for requests inside this window; the employee must use a personal vehicle or the transport desk's standby cab.

### 2.2 Cancellation
A booking can be cancelled up to 1 hour before the scheduled time without any consequence.

## 3. Night shift and safety

### 3.1 Security escort
A security escort is mandatory for women employees on drops after 10:00 PM. Drops before 10:00 PM do not carry an escort requirement.

### 3.2 Night drop timings
Night drops run on shared routes. A woman employee must be the last person dropped on a shared route only if an escort is not available.

### 3.3 Safety reporting
Safety incidents must be reported to the Borealis security desk within 24 hours.

## 4. Weekends and holidays

### 4.1 Weekend transport
Regular transport is not available on Saturday or Sunday. Weekend transport is provided only for employees on an approved project roster signed by the transport administrator.

### 4.2 Public holidays
There is no transport on public holidays. Employees working on a holiday must use the reimbursed commute allowance instead.

## 5. Ad-hoc trips and approvals

### 5.1 Ad-hoc trip approval
Ad-hoc trips are approved by the transport administrator, not by the reporting manager.

### 5.2 Airport trips
Airport pickups and drops are approved by the transport administrator. The trip must be booked 48 hours in advance.

### 5.3 Cost cap
Any cab trip is capped at Rs 1,000 per trip. Trips above the cap are not reimbursed.

## 6. Guests

### 6.1 Guest travel
Guests may travel on shuttles when the host employee requests it at least 24 hours in advance and the transport administrator approves. Guests are not allowed in night-shift cabs.

## 7. No-show and penalties

### 7.1 No-show penalty
There is no monetary penalty for a no-show. The first and second no-shows in any rolling 30 days result in a written reminder.

### 7.2 Repeated no-shows
On the third no-show in a rolling 30 days, transport is suspended for 7 days.

## 9. Purpose, scope and definitions

### 9.1 Purpose of this policy
The purpose of this policy is to establish a clear framework that governs the provision of shared transport services for employees engaged in night routes and project-based rosters, ensuring consistency, safety, and fiscal responsibility across all relevant operations. It delineates the responsibilities of the transport administrator, the security desk, host employees, and reporting managers, and aligns their actions with the eligibility criteria, timing constraints, and security requirements set out in the applicable sections of the document. By referencing the specific provisions concerning distance, shift parameters, cut-off times, cancellation procedures, escort obligations, night-drop schedules, safety reporting, weekend and public-holiday arrangements, ad-hoc approvals, airport travel, cost limitations, guest travel, and penalties for non-attendance, the policy provides a comprehensive guide that supports operational efficiency while safeguarding employee welfare. This purpose statement reinforces the organization's commitment to transparent, accountable, and secure transport solutions, and it serves as the foundational reference for all subsequent sections that detail the scope, definitions, and procedural requirements.

### 9.2 Scope and applicability
The transport policy applies to all employees of Borealis Systems who require conveyance for official duties, including those assigned to shared night routes, project-based rosters, and ad-hoc assignments. Eligibility for use of the service is governed by the criteria set out in the distance-based and shift-based provisions, and the policy extends to any travel that falls within the operational windows defined in the cut-off and cancellation sections. All transport requests must be processed through the transport administrator, and any deviation from the standard procedures, such as the need for a security escort or night-drop timing, must be authorized in accordance with the relevant sections.

The scope further encompasses weekend and public-holiday travel, airport transfers, and guest movements, each of which is subject to the specific approvals and cost limitations detailed in the corresponding sections. Managers are responsible for ensuring compliance with the no-show penalties and repeated-no-show provisions, while the security desk must enforce safety reporting requirements. Administrative notes and record-keeping obligations are outlined to support consistent application across all divisions.

### 9.3 Definitions of common terms
The purpose of this document is to establish a shared understanding of the terminology employed throughout the transport policy, thereby ensuring consistent interpretation and application by all responsible parties. "Transport administrator" denotes the individual tasked with coordinating vehicle allocation, schedule adherence, and record maintenance, as outlined in the relevant procedural sections. "Borealis security desk" refers to the central point of contact for verification of personnel clearance and the provision of escort services in accordance with the security provisions set forth. "Host employee" identifies the staff member who arranges and oversees the receipt of a traveler at the destination, while "reporting manager" designates the supervisor responsible for approving travel requests and monitoring compliance with eligibility criteria.

The term "eligible trip" encompasses any journey that satisfies the distance and shift parameters defined in the eligibility sections, and which conforms to the timing and cancellation requirements specified elsewhere. "Ad-hoc trip" describes a request that falls outside routine scheduling and must receive approval as stipulated in the appropriate approval section. "No-show" characterises a failure to appear for a scheduled pickup, invoking the penalties described in the relevant enforcement sections. These definitions provide the foundation for interpreting all subsequent provisions within the policy.

## 10. Roles and responsibilities

### 10.1 Responsibilities of employees
Employees are required to adhere to the eligibility criteria and timing constraints that govern transport usage, as set out in sections one point one and one point two, and to observe the cut-off provisions described in section two point one. When a scheduled journey must be altered, the cancellation process outlined in section two point two must be followed without delay, and any deviation from the approved itinerary must be reported in accordance with the safety reporting requirements of section three point three. All travel that occurs outside regular operating hours shall be coordinated with the security desk, ensuring that the escort protocol of section three point one and the night-drop timing rules of section three point two are observed.

Host employees must confirm the accuracy of travel requests and obtain the necessary approvals as stipulated in sections five point one and five point two, while also respecting the cost limitations defined in section five point three. Reporting managers are responsible for monitoring compliance, addressing any instances of non-attendance in line with sections seven point one and seven point two, and documenting administrative actions as required by section eight point one. The transport administrator shall maintain records, facilitate communication among stakeholders, and ensure that all procedures are applied consistently across the organization.

### 10.2 Responsibilities of reporting managers
The reporting manager bears primary accountability for ensuring that all transport requests submitted by their team conform to the eligibility criteria outlined in sections one point one and one point two. Prior to approval, the manager must verify that the proposed travel aligns with the cut-off timings specified in section two point one and that any required security escort is arranged in accordance with section three point one. Where a request involves night-time drop-offs, the manager is required to confirm compliance with the parameters set out in section three point two and to document any safety concerns as directed in section three point three.

In addition, the reporting manager must oversee the proper handling of cancellations as described in section two point two, and ensure that any ad-hoc trips receive the authorization mandated in section five point one. When travel coincides with weekend periods or public holidays, the manager must reference sections four point one and four point two to determine eligibility. Finally, the manager is responsible for recording all transport activity in the administrative system as required by section eight point one, and for communicating any deviations to the transport administrator promptly.

### 10.3 Responsibilities of the transport desk
The transport desk shall act as the central coordination point for all movement requests, ensuring that each submission is logged, verified against the eligibility criteria set out in section 1.1 and section 1.2, and routed to the appropriate approving authority. Upon receipt of a request, the desk must confirm that the proposed timing complies with the cut-off parameters described in section 2.1, and must flag any deviation for immediate review. All cancellations must be processed in accordance with the procedures outlined in section 2.2, and any required security escort shall be arranged as mandated by section 3.1, with particular attention to the night-drop windows specified in section 3.2.

The desk is also responsible for maintaining accurate records of all transport activities, including the documentation of safety incidents as required by section 3.3 and the reporting of weekend or public-holiday travel in line with section 4.1 and section 4.2. For any ad-hoc or airport-related journeys, the desk must secure the necessary approvals pursuant to section 5.1 and section 5.2, while ensuring that the cost limitations of section 5.3 are observed. Finally, the desk shall communicate any no-show penalties as defined in section 7.1 and monitor repeated infractions in accordance with section 7.2, recording all actions in the administrative log referenced in section 8.1.

### 10.4 Responsibilities of HR and administration
The Human Resources function, together with the transport administration team, is charged with overseeing the implementation of the eligibility criteria set out in sections one point one and one point two, ensuring that all staff classifications are correctly recorded and that any changes to work patterns are promptly reflected in the transport roster. HR must maintain up-to-date records of employee status, coordinate with the security desk to verify compliance with the escort requirements of section three point one, and confirm that night-drop timings adhere to the parameters described in section three point two. In addition, the administration office is responsible for monitoring the cut-off timings of section two point one, processing cancellations in line with section two point two, and managing the approval workflow for ad-hoc journeys as required by section five point one, while observing the cost limitations outlined in section five point three.

Both functions shall collaborate to ensure that weekend and public-holiday transport provisions, as detailed in sections four point one and four point two, are communicated clearly to all eligible participants. They are also tasked with recording safety incidents in accordance with section three point three, handling guest travel arrangements per section six point one, and applying the no-show penalties described in sections seven point one and seven point two. All administrative notes and updates must be logged as prescribed in section eight point one, and any deviations are to be escalated to the reporting manager for corrective action.

In addition, special considerations apply to weekend and public holiday travel, which must be evaluated against the dedicated weekend and holiday provisions. Any ad-hoc or airport-related movement requires prior approval as outlined in the ad-hoc trip and airport trip sections, and must respect the cost limitation defined in the cost cap clause. ### 11.3 Pick-up and drop points

The vehicle standards and inspection protocol establishes that all transport assets must conform to the safety and performance criteria defined by the organization, with compliance verified through a systematic review process. Each vehicle shall be maintained in a condition that satisfies the requirements set out in the relevant sections, and any deviation from the prescribed condition must be reported in accordance with the procedures described in section three point three. The transport administrator is responsible for coordinating the inspection timetable and confirming that all records are up to date, while the security desk must verify that the vehicle meets the escort criteria outlined in section three point one prior to deployment on night operations. Should a vehicle fail to meet the established standards, it shall be withdrawn from service until corrective actions are completed and the inspection is re-validated, thereby upholding the organization's commitment to safety and reliability.

The transport administrator shall ensure that every driver engaged for Borealis Systems' shared night routes or project-based rosters completes the verification process before being assigned to a vehicle. Verification includes confirmation of a valid commercial licence, a clean driving record, and successful completion of the background check prescribed in the security protocol. Onboarding of a newly verified driver requires the submission of all supporting documentation to the administrative office, followed by a briefing on the operational standards set forth in the shift-based eligibility and night drop timing sections. Once the driver has acknowledged receipt of the policy documents and signed the compliance form, the administrator may activate the driver's status in the system, thereby permitting participation in weekend transport, public holiday assignments, and any ad-hoc trips that fall within the approved cost cap. All steps must be documented in accordance with the administrative note requirement.

The organization expects every driver to uphold the highest standards of professionalism, safety and courtesy while representing Borealis Systems on all routes and assignments. All drivers shall complete the mandatory training program approved by the transport administrator before undertaking any shift, and must maintain current certification as required by applicable regulations. Ongoing competency is verified through periodic assessments, and any deficiencies identified must be remedied in accordance with the procedures outlined in section eight point one.

Drivers are required to observe all security protocols, including the presence of an escort where stipulated in section three point one, and to adhere strictly to the night-drop timing requirements set out in section three point two. Any incident, near miss or safety concern must be reported promptly following the guidelines of section three point three, and failure to do so may result in disciplinary action. The transport administrator, in collaboration with the security desk, will monitor compliance and may suspend driving privileges pending investigation, as referenced in section seven point one and section seven point two.

## 14. Safety and security procedures

### 14.1 How the security escort is deployed
The deployment of a security escort shall be coordinated by the transport administrator in accordance with the provisions set out in section three point one. When a request is logged, the administrator must verify the eligibility of the employee and confirm that the escort aligns with the operational requirements of the night route or project roster. The escort will be assigned to accompany the host employee from the point of departure to the designated drop location, ensuring compliance with the timing constraints described in section two point one and the safety reporting obligations of section three point three.

All escort assignments shall be documented in the transport management system and communicated to the reporting manager prior to the commencement of travel. Any deviation from the planned escort schedule must be reported immediately to the security desk, and corrective action shall follow the procedures outlined in section seven point one. The security desk retains authority to adjust escort deployment in response to emergent risks, with any such adjustments recorded as an administrative note pursuant to section eight point one.

### 14.2 Emergency helpline and panic alerts
The Emergency Helpline and Panic Alerts protocol is a critical component of the Safety and Security Procedures framework and applies to all personnel engaged in shared night routes or project-based rosters. All employees must be aware that the helpline number is to be used exclusively for genuine emergencies that threaten personal safety, vehicle security, or the integrity of the transport operation. Activation of a panic alert through the designated mobile application will automatically notify the Borealis security desk, the transport administrator, and the reporting manager, triggering the response sequence outlined in the relevant security escort provision referenced in section three point one. The system records the incident and initiates the safety reporting process as required by section three point three, ensuring that each event is documented and reviewed in accordance with established investigative standards.

The policy further stipulates that any misuse of the emergency channel, including non-emergency testing or frivolous alerts, will be subject to the disciplinary measures described in section seven point one and the escalation procedures of section seven point two. All staff are required to familiarize themselves with the operational guidelines and to confirm receipt of this directive through the administrative note mechanism set out in section eight point one, thereby affirming their commitment to maintaining a secure transport environment.

### 14.3 Incident investigation process
The Incident Investigation Process is governed by the principles set out in the Safety and Security Procedures chapter and must be initiated promptly whenever a report is logged under the safety reporting provision referenced in section three point three. The transport administrator shall coordinate the collection of factual data, secure any relevant documentation, and arrange for an interview with the host employee and any witnesses, ensuring that the security desk is informed in accordance with the escort requirement of section three point one. All findings shall be compiled into a formal report that is submitted to the reporting manager for review, with reference to the applicable cut-off times described in section two point one and the cancellation protocol of section two point two.

The reporting manager is responsible for evaluating the investigation outcomes, determining corrective actions, and communicating any necessary adjustments to the weekend transport guidelines of section four point one or the public holiday provisions of section four point two, as appropriate. Documentation of the investigation shall be retained in accordance with the administrative note requirements of section eight point one and shall be made available for audit in line with the ad-hoc trip approval process of section five point one.

### 14.4 Safety review committee
The Safety Review Committee is convened under the authority of the Safety and Security Procedures chapter to provide ongoing oversight of all transport-related risk management activities. The committee is composed of the transport administrator, a representative from the security desk, a host employee selected from the operational roster, and the reporting manager for each project. Its mandate includes evaluating compliance with the eligibility criteria set out in section one point one and section one point two, reviewing the timing parameters defined in section two point one, and ensuring that any security escort requirements described in section three point one are consistently applied. The committee also monitors the implementation of night drop timing controls from section three point two and the safety reporting mechanisms of section three point three, recommending corrective actions where deviations are identified.

All findings and recommendations are documented in an administrative note as required by section eight point one and are communicated to senior management for final approval. The committee meets at intervals determined by the applicable cut-off established in section two point one, and it retains the authority to refer any non-conformities to the appropriate governance body for further review. This structured approach ensures that transport operations remain aligned with the organization's broader safety objectives and regulatory obligations.

In the event that a medical emergency arises while a participant is engaged in a scheduled journey, the transport administrator shall be notified immediately and shall activate the protocol outlined in the relevant security and safety sections. The administrator must coordinate with the security desk to arrange an escort as required by the provisions of section three point one, and ensure that the vehicle proceeds to the nearest appropriate medical facility without undue delay. The host employee and reporting manager are responsible for communicating the situation to the affected individual's designated contacts and for facilitating any necessary follow-up arrangements, including the provision of transport for subsequent appointments if the circumstances fall within the eligibility criteria described in sections one point one and one point two. Any deviation from the standard itinerary caused by the emergency shall be treated as an exception under the ad-hoc approval process referenced in section five point one, and any associated costs shall be evaluated against the limit set in section five point three.

### 15.2 Support for employees with disabilities
The organization recognises that employees with disabilities may require additional assistance to participate fully in shared night routes and project-based rosters. When a request is received, the administrator will consult the applicable cut-off guidelines and, where appropriate, seek approval in accordance with the ad-hoc trip approval process, while maintaining confidentiality and respecting the employee's privacy.

All adjustments, including but not limited to modified pick-up points, specialised vehicle equipment, or the provision of a security escort, shall be documented in the administrative record as required. The employee may request a review of the arrangement at any time, and the request will be evaluated against the existing security escort and night drop timing provisions. ### 15.3 Support during pregnancy and recovery
The organization recognises that employees who are expecting a child or recovering from childbirth may require adjustments to their transport arrangements in order to maintain safety, comfort and punctuality. The transport administrator shall coordinate with the host employee and the reporting manager to evaluate any required modifications, ensuring that any alteration aligns with the eligibility criteria set out in sections one point one and one point two. Where a change in schedule is necessary, the revised timing shall respect the cut-off provisions described in section two point one, and any cancellation must follow the process outlined in section two point two.

In circumstances where a security escort is ordinarily required, the need for such accompaniment shall be assessed in accordance with section three point one, while night-time drop considerations shall be guided by section three point two. All incidents or concerns arising from the adjusted travel must be documented pursuant to the safety reporting framework in section three point three. The policy further obliges the transport administrator to seek approval for any ad-hoc travel related to medical appointments as stipulated in section five point one, and to ensure that any cost implications remain within the limit set in section five point three.

## 16. Disruptions and contingency planning

### 16.1 Weather and flooding
The organization recognises that severe weather and flooding constitute a primary source of disruption to scheduled transport services and therefore mandates that any anticipated or actual adverse conditions be reported immediately to the transport administrator. In accordance with the procedures set out in section two point one, the applicable cut-off for notifying the transport administrator shall be observed, and any deviation from the planned itinerary must be communicated without delay. When flooding renders a route impassable, the transport administrator shall consult the security desk to determine whether an alternative secure pathway is available, as required by section three point one, and shall adjust night drop timings in line with the guidance of section three point two.

Should the conditions exceed the thresholds defined in the relevant operational guidelines, the transport administrator is authorised to suspend service and arrange for alternative conveyance, subject to the approval process described in section five point one. All such actions shall be documented in the administrative note prescribed by section eight point one, and any safety concerns arising from the weather event must be reported pursuant to section three point three. This approach ensures continuity of service while safeguarding personnel and assets during periods of extreme weather.

### 16.2 Strikes, curfews and public disturbances
The transport administrator shall monitor any industrial action, curfew imposition or public disturbance that could affect scheduled routes and shall consult the security desk to assess risk and determine whether alternative arrangements are required. When a disruption is identified, the administrator must reference the applicable eligibility criteria set out in sections one point one and one point two, and must verify that any deviation from the normal schedule complies with the cut-off provisions described in section two point one. Should a deviation be necessary, the administrator shall obtain the requisite approval in accordance with section five point one and shall document the decision in the administrative record as required by section eight point one.

In the event that a curfew or disturbance restricts travel during night hours, the security desk shall coordinate with the host employee and reporting manager to ensure that any required escort or adjusted drop timing adheres to the requirements of sections three point one and three point two. All incidents must be reported through the safety reporting channel outlined in section three point three, and any additional costs incurred shall be evaluated against the limit set in section five point three.

### 16.3 Vehicle breakdown during a trip
When a vehicle experiences a mechanical failure while transporting an employee, the transport administrator must be notified immediately and must activate the contingency protocol outlined in the relevant operational guidelines. The administrator shall arrange for a replacement vehicle or an alternative mode of conveyance that complies with the eligibility criteria described in section one point one and section one point two, ensuring that the employee reaches the intended destination without undue delay. In circumstances where the breakdown occurs after the applicable cut-off time, the administrator must also observe the timing requirements set out in section two point one, and any necessary adjustments to the schedule shall be coordinated with the reporting manager.

The security desk is responsible for confirming the safety of the stranded employee and, if required, providing a security escort in accordance with section three point one. All incidents must be documented promptly, and a safety report shall be filed as prescribed in section three point three. The report shall include details of the breakdown, actions taken, and any impact on cost considerations referenced in section five point three. This documentation will be reviewed as part of the administrative note process described in section eight point one.

### 16.4 Business continuity for the transport service
The transport service shall remain operational under all foreseeable disruptions, and the procedures set forth in this chapter provide a clear framework for maintaining continuity. When an incident arises that could affect scheduled routes, the transport administrator must consult the eligibility criteria outlined in sections one point one and one point two to determine which journeys may be prioritized. Any deviation from the standard timetable shall be communicated promptly to the host employee and the reporting manager, with reference to the cut-off provisions described in section two point one, ensuring that all parties are aware of revised departure windows and that security escort requirements from section three point one are upheld where applicable.

In the event of a systemic interruption, such as a network outage or severe weather, the Borealis security desk shall activate the contingency protocol, invoking the weekend and public holiday provisions of sections four point one and four point two as needed. All ad-hoc approvals must follow the process in section five point one, and any cost implications shall remain within the limit set in section five point three. Documentation of the incident and corrective actions shall be recorded in accordance with the safety reporting guidelines of section three point three and the administrative note requirements of section eight point one, thereby preserving auditability and supporting future resilience planning.

The conduct of all personnel while occupying company-provided or shared vehicles shall reflect the professional standards expected of Borealis Systems employees and shall be consistent with the provisions set out in the broader conduct, discipline and grievances chapter. Each individual is required to maintain a courteous demeanor, refrain from any activity that could impair safe operation, and ensure that personal devices are used in a manner that does not distract the driver, as outlined in the applicable safety reporting requirements of section three point three. Any deviation from these expectations, including the consumption of prohibited substances or the failure to adhere to the established night drop timings of section three point two, shall be addressed in accordance with the disciplinary procedures referenced in section seven point one and section seven point two.

Furthermore, employees must respect the security protocols mandated by section three point one, cooperate fully with any escort arrangements, and promptly report any incidents or concerns through the channels described in section three point three. Failure to comply with these conduct obligations may result in corrective action as detailed in the administrative note of section eight point one, ensuring that the integrity of Borealis Systems' transport operations remains uncompromised.

### 17.2 Disciplinary process for violations
The disciplinary process for any breach of the transport policy is governed by the principles set out in the Conduct, Discipline and Grievances chapter and must be applied consistently by the transport administrator in coordination with the reporting manager. Upon identification of a violation, the incident shall be documented in accordance with the administrative note requirements of section eight point one, and the employee concerned will be notified of the alleged breach and invited to provide a written response. The response, together with any supporting evidence, will be reviewed by the transport administrator and the reporting manager, who will determine whether the matter warrants formal disciplinary action as defined in the relevant corporate guidelines.

If formal action is deemed appropriate, the employee will receive a written notice outlining the nature of the violation, the specific policy reference, and the proposed sanction, which may range from a formal warning to more severe measures in line with the organization's progressive discipline framework. The employee retains the right to appeal the decision in accordance with the grievance procedures described elsewhere, and the final determination will be recorded in the employee's personnel file, ensuring transparency and compliance with all applicable sections referenced herein.

### 17.3 Grievance redressal
The grievance redressal process is overseen by the transport administrator in coordination with the reporting manager and the security desk, ensuring that any concerns raised by host employees are addressed promptly and impartially. All submissions must be recorded in the administrative note as outlined in section eight point one, and the complainant will receive acknowledgment within the applicable cut-off period defined in section two point one. An initial review will be conducted to determine whether the issue falls under the scope of existing eligibility criteria, such as those described in sections one point one and one point two, or whether it pertains to security escort provisions in section three point one, night drop timing in section three point two, or any other relevant operational parameter.

If the matter requires further investigation, it will be escalated to the appropriate managerial level, with a formal response provided in accordance with the cancellation guidelines of section two point two and the no-show penalties set out in sections seven point one and seven point two. The final resolution will be documented, and any corrective actions will be implemented consistent with the cost cap provisions of section five point three and the ad-hoc trip approval process of section five point one, thereby maintaining the integrity of the transport programme and safeguarding employee confidence.

### 17.4 Escalation matrix
The escalation matrix for conduct, discipline and grievances is designed to provide a clear and timely pathway for resolution, ensuring that all concerns are addressed with appropriate authority and documentation. Initial concerns shall be raised with the immediate reporting manager, who is responsible for assessing the issue against the standards set out in the relevant sections and for determining whether further escalation is required. Should the matter remain unresolved, the next level of review involves the transport administrator, who will coordinate with the security desk and reference the provisions of section three point one and section three point two as necessary, while also ensuring that any safety reporting obligations under section three point three are fulfilled.

If the dispute persists beyond this stage, it shall be escalated to the senior management review panel, which will evaluate the case in the context of the broader policy framework, including the weekend and public holiday provisions of sections four point one and four point two, and the cost considerations of section five point three. All actions taken at each tier must be recorded in accordance with the administrative note requirements of section eight point one, and the outcome shall be communicated to the host employee and any other affected parties in a timely and professional manner.

## 18. Cost management and audit

### 18.1 Billing and reconciliation with vendors
The Finance Office shall oversee all vendor invoicing related to transportation services, ensuring that each submission aligns with the cost parameters established in the applicable cost cap reference. All invoices must be cross-checked against the approved trip authorisations documented in the ad-hoc trip approval record and the airport trips register, with any deviation reported to the transport administrator for immediate review. Reconciliation shall be performed on a regular cycle, and any discrepancy identified shall be escalated in accordance with the administrative note guidance, while maintaining a clear audit trail for future verification.

Payments to external providers will be processed only after confirmation that the service delivery complies with the security escort requirements and night drop timings as set out in the relevant sections. The finance team shall retain supporting documentation for the period stipulated in the cost management and audit chapter, and shall make this material available to internal auditors upon request. Should any invoice exceed the limit set in the cost cap provision, the finance office must obtain written approval from the reporting manager before authorising settlement, thereby preserving fiscal responsibility and contractual compliance.

### 18.2 Cost allocation to departments
The allocation of transportation expenses to departmental budgets shall be governed by the principles set out in the cost management and audit chapter, ensuring that each cost centre bears the financial responsibility for travel that directly supports its operational objectives. All charges arising from shared night routes, project-based rosters, and ad-hoc trips shall be recorded against the department of the host employee, as defined in the relevant eligibility provisions, and reconciled in accordance with the approval process described in the appropriate section. When a trip is classified under the airport travel category, the cost allocation shall follow the guidelines established in the designated section, and any expense exceeding the limit set in the cost cap provision shall be subject to further review before departmental charging.

Any deviation from the standard allocation methodology must be documented and justified in an administrative note, referencing the specific sections that govern eligibility, cut-off timings, and cancellation policies. The transport administrator shall ensure that all allocations are reflected in the monthly financial statements and that any adjustments required by the security escort or safety reporting provisions are incorporated promptly. Continuous audit of these allocations will be performed to verify compliance with the overarching cost management framework.

### 18.3 Internal audit and compliance review
The internal audit and compliance review function serves to verify that all transport expenditures conform to the parameters established throughout this policy, with particular emphasis on the financial thresholds described in section five point three and the approval procedures outlined in section five point one. The transport administrator, in coordination with the corporate audit team, shall conduct a systematic examination of each transaction on a quarterly basis, cross-referencing receipts, mileage logs, and security escort records as required by section three point one. Any deviation from the authorized cost structure or unauthorized use of resources shall be documented and escalated to the reporting manager for corrective action, ensuring that the organization maintains fiscal responsibility and adheres to the governance standards set forth in section eight point one.

Findings from each audit cycle will be compiled into a comprehensive report that highlights compliance levels, identifies recurring variances, and recommends procedural enhancements. This report shall be reviewed by senior management and retained in the central repository for a period consistent with regulatory obligations. Continuous improvement is achieved through periodic updates to the cost-control framework, informed by the audit outcomes and aligned with the overarching objectives of the cost management and audit chapter.

## 19. Governance and administration

### 19.1 Policy ownership
The governance of the transport programme rests with the appointed transport administrator, who is responsible for maintaining the policy register, ensuring that all procedural references are current, and coordinating updates with the Borealis security desk and the reporting manager. The administrator shall oversee the implementation of the eligibility criteria set out in section one point one and section one point two, and shall verify that all cut-off provisions described in section two point one are applied consistently across shared night routes and project-based rosters. Any amendment to the security escort requirements of section three point one, the night-drop timing rules of section three point two, or the safety reporting obligations of section three point three must be documented by the administrator and communicated to all host employees through the established administrative note process of section eight point one.

Responsibility for monitoring compliance with weekend transport provisions of section four point one, public-holiday arrangements of section four point two, and ad-hoc trip approvals of section five point one rests jointly with the transport administrator and the reporting manager. The administrator shall retain records of all approvals, cancellations, and cost-cap considerations referenced in section five point three, and shall ensure that any guest travel requests outlined in section six point one are processed in accordance with the overarching governance framework.

### 19.2 Review and amendment process
The Review and Amendment Process for the transport policy shall be overseen by the designated transport administrator in collaboration with the Borealis security desk and the reporting manager. Any proposed modification shall be documented in an administrative note as required by section eight point one, and shall reference the specific core sections that are affected, such as eligibility criteria, timing cut-offs, security escort provisions, weekend and holiday provisions, ad-hoc approvals, cost limitations, guest travel rules, and penalty structures. The draft amendment shall be circulated to all relevant stakeholders for comment, and the transport administrator shall compile the feedback and present a consolidated recommendation to senior governance for final approval.

Amendments shall become effective only after the formal sign-off documented in the administrative record and shall be communicated to all employees through the established channels. The transport administrator shall maintain a revision history in accordance with section eight point one, ensuring that each change is traceable to its originating proposal and the governing authority that authorized it. This process guarantees that the policy remains current, compliant, and aligned with operational requirements while providing clear accountability for any adjustments.

### 19.3 Handling exceptions to this policy
The governance framework permits the transport administrator to consider exceptional circumstances that fall outside the standard eligibility and timing criteria, provided that any deviation is documented in accordance with the administrative note outlined in section eight point one. When a request originates from the security desk or a host employee and involves a scenario not covered by the distance or shift parameters of sections one point one and one point two, the administrator must obtain written confirmation from the reporting manager before authorising the exception, and the decision must reference the relevant cut-off provisions of section two point one.

All approved exceptions shall be recorded in the central log and reviewed periodically to ensure alignment with the cost limitations described in section five point three and the safety obligations of section three point three. Should an exception relate to weekend or public holiday travel, the administrator must also verify compliance with sections four point one and four point two, and any ad-hoc approval must follow the process set out in section five point one. This approach maintains consistency while allowing flexibility for legitimate, documented needs.

### 19.4 Related documents and forms
The Governance and Administration chapter includes a dedicated segment that identifies all supporting documentation and required forms for the transport programme. The related documents list comprises the master eligibility matrix, the timing and cancellation schedule, the security escort protocol, the night-drop timetable, the safety incident log, the weekend and public-holiday transport guide, the ad-hoc trip request template, the airport travel checklist, the cost-control ceiling worksheet, the guest travel authorization, the no-show remediation notice, the repeated-no-show escalation form, and the administrative record-keeping note. Each item is cross-referenced to the appropriate policy clause, for example the eligibility matrix aligns with the provisions set out in section one point one and section one point two, while the timing schedule mirrors the requirements of section two point one and section two point two. The security escort protocol is linked to section three point one, and the night-drop timetable corresponds to section three point two. All forms must be completed by the transport administrator and retained in accordance with the record-keeping standards described in section eight point one, ensuring consistent auditability and compliance across the organisation.

### 19.5 Contact points
The governance and administration chapter designates clear contact points to ensure consistent handling of transport matters across all operational scenarios. All requests, modifications, and inquiries shall be directed to the transport administrator, who is responsible for maintaining the master schedule, processing approvals as outlined in the relevant sections, and coordinating with the security desk for any escort requirements referenced in the appropriate clause. For issues that arise during night operations, the Borealis security desk shall serve as the immediate point of contact, providing real-time assistance and escalating matters to the host employee when a passenger requires on-site support as stipulated in the designated sections.

When a reporting manager identifies a need for an exception or wishes to initiate an ad-hoc trip, the request must be routed through the transport administrator, who will verify compliance with the approval process and cost parameters set forth in the applicable sections. All communications shall be documented in the administrative note system to preserve an audit trail, and any deviations from the established procedures shall be reported in accordance with the safety reporting requirements. This structured approach ensures accountability, timely response, and alignment with the overarching transport policy.
